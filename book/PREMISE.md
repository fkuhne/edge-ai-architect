## Angle

The subject alone — learning edge ML — is crowded. What makes this particular telling worth reading is the angle, and there are at least two available here. React to these, pick one, or find a third:

**A. The constraint as the teacher.** Almost every ML book assumes a cloud GPU or at least a serious machine. This one is done entirely on an 8 GB laptop, and the argument is that the limit is pedagogically *better*: you cannot avoid learning about memory, quantization, and latency budgets when you have no headroom to hide in. Fits the material exactly, and edge deployment is where the industry is going anyway.

**B. Learning a hard technical subject with an AI, honestly.** The meta-layer. This project already contains a real, documented moment where the AI did the work and had to be told to stop — and the recovery from that is more instructive than most writing on the topic, which is either uncritical or dismissive. Very few people have written this well. Possibly the more valuable book; also the one that dates fastest.

**A+B** is plausible — the constraint story as the spine, the AI-mentorship story as a recurring thread — but be suspicious of that answer, because "both" is usually how a book loses its shape.

## Practical decisions

**Language.** Portuguese or English? Worth settling early, because it applies to every log entry from here on. Consider: raw thinking is often more honest in your first language, but if the book is in English, logs in Portuguese mean translating everything later — and translation quietly sands off exactly the rough texture that made the logs worth keeping. Mixing is allowed and probably fine for raw entries.
